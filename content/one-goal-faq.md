## The partnership

**What is the OneGoal and Willow partnership? (Settled)** OneGoal is moving its student, teacher, and administrator technology onto Willow's platform, and the two organizations are co-designing one 9-12 curriculum and going to market together on OneGoal's new whole-school model. It is a three-year co-development agreement. OneGoal's board approved it Aug 13, 2026; a memorandum of understanding was signed in August; the definitive agreement is targeted for mid-October.

**Is this a merger or an acquisition? (Settled)** No. OneGoal and Willow stay separate organizations. OneGoal keeps full ownership of its district relationships, brand, and revenue. Willow is the technology and curriculum partner.

**What does each side bring? (Settled)**

- OneGoal: twenty years of implementation expertise, the Quality Enrollment framework and its evidence base, the advising model, curriculum, Year 13 postsecondary success advising, the partner footprint in six regions, DecidED, and the relationship with every district.
- Willow: the platform (curriculum delivery, career exploration, best-fit list building, progress monitoring, scheduling), a 400-lesson 9-12 library grounded in purpose development, career exploration, and postsecondary planning, labor-market data and ROI, Alma (the AI advisor and guided-reflection tool), and a mission-driven team that ships quickly.

**Why did OneGoal choose to buy rather than keep building? (Settled)** OneGoal buys technology before it builds it, and built Studio, MyOptions, and DecidED only because the market had nothing good enough. The new program needs four things the old stack cannot do: modular delivery with dosage tracking (Canvas cannot deliver the program in modules or track how much a student completed), deeper career content than O\*NET alone, one front door instead of many sign-ons, and interoperability with the systems schools already run. Willow is the first platform that meets OneGoal's bar for quality and is willing to build the Quality Enrollment framework into the tool itself.

**How do we describe it to a district? (Settled)** OneGoal's transformation leads. OneGoal can now support whole-school and whole-district outcomes. The talking points below are taken from OneGoal's own materials (the [Program Schema deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1zDuajhK8aD3FEMvEsF7xF5sJYAkjY6YG3KM6HQfzssc/edit) and the 2031 strategic plan):

- The problem: counselor-to-student ratios run as high as 400:1, leaving students an average of 38 minutes of postsecondary support across all of high school. Only 13 percent of teens feel fully prepared to decide what to do after graduation, and guidance is often based on outdated anecdotes rather than real-time data.
- Our value: OneGoal uniquely builds lasting school capacity to increase key high school and postsecondary student outcomes. We equip educators to become high-quality advisors so that all students find their purpose, explore different pathways, and persist to achieve their college and career goals.
- How: we work with schools to embed and institutionalize a coherent, high-quality postsecondary classroom experience directly into the master schedule.
- What we offer: a proven approach built on decades of experience with verified results; school-wide reach that fits a variety of schedules, serves students as early as 9th grade, and scales to entire high schools; relevant, future-focused preparation for many paths in a changing workforce; purpose first, so students set career goals and choose the path with the best chance of success; a structured 9-13 transition that prioritizes persistence into life after high school; and teacher empowerment, mobilizing your educators within the school day instead of outsourcing.
- Evidence: UChicago's Inclusive Economy Lab found OneGoal Fellows are 46 percent more likely to enroll, 47 percent more likely to persist, and 40 percent more likely to graduate than similar peers.
- Positioning: a turnkey partner that complements stretched counseling capacity rather than replacing it, building lasting internal capacity through a gradual-release arc.
- The partnership: Willow is the one-stop shop for our student-, teacher-, and administrator-facing technology, so OneGoal keeps full ownership of district relationships, brand, and revenue while delivering a stronger classroom experience and better interoperability with school systems. Willow is the enabling partner, named as helpful and credited, never the subject. Co-branded, not white-label. "Willow is how we do it, not the value proposition" (Monique, Sept 18).

**Is OneGoal narrowing to advisory only? (Settled)** No. The course-based classroom experience, taught in the school day by the school's own educators, stays central. Advisory is one door in.

**What are Quality Enrollment and Opportunity Advising? (Working)** Quality Enrollment (QE) is OneGoal's framework: students should complete the right pathway, not just enroll anywhere, judged on personal fit, financial fit, academic match, and completion supports. The full framework is in OneGoal's [Quality Enrollment Framework deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1lOS64l6fzEqJJs_PNt8NPBm2-aTR4OaeEjFGc-NmiTM/edit). Opportunity Advising is the broader field movement, with TNTP as a likely co-founding partner alongside OneGoal and Willow, and is sequenced as a second announcement after the partnership itself.

**When can I talk about the partnership publicly? (Settled)** Not before the definitive agreement is signed (target Oct 15-17). The announcement is planned for signing day, with a pre-announcement waterfall so no funder, board member, legislator, or partner learns it from a press release. National and regional announcements run in parallel; each ED tailors the message to local pain (Texas HB8 and CCMR, Colorado's Big 3, MA MyCAP). Until then, route any external question to Monique or Jenny. The announcement is a great opportunity to call your best 10-15 leads and funders to give them a heads-up beforehand and schedule a deeper dive.

**What can I say to partners and funders before the announcement? (Working)** In a private conversation it is fine to say OneGoal is exploring a partnership with Willow, which matches OneGoal's current internal talking points. Nothing public, and no press, social posts, or conference mentions, until signing. Keep the tone modest: this is OneGoal's program evolving, not a big technology story, and the headline is never Willow.

**Does anything change for our current partners this school year? (Settled)** No. This school year runs as planned on the current model and tools. Current partners hear about the new model through their conversion conversation (January to March), not through the announcement.

## Product and curriculum

**What exactly is "the Classroom Experience"? (Settled)** The core offering in the Program Schema: labor-market-informed career exploration and postsecondary planning embedded in the daily master schedule, taught by the school's own educators. Five components:

1. 9-12 curriculum: modular units that adapt to the partner's master schedule with grade-level flexibility, grounded in the Quality Enrollment framework and ROI data.
2. Technology stack: one student- and educator-facing platform for curriculum, career exploration, and advising; a scheduling tool so the curriculum can be modularized per partner; rostering and interoperability with school systems where possible.
3. Training and support: kickoff and ongoing training, live and async; data-driven supports that let one OneGoal staff member support a caseload of schools.
4. Progress monitoring: a school lead tracks participation and outcomes across the school; automated reports go to leadership through the year.
5. Postsecondary success advising (Year 13): hybrid advising through the first year after graduation, with enrollment and persistence data reported back to school leadership.

**What do students achieve by grade? (Working; schema decision dial at 75%)**

| Grade | Theme | Career discovery | Fit and pathway | Affordability and enrollment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 9 | Lay the Foundation | Explores interests through guided self-assessment | Understands the range of pathways | Understands how GPA, coursework, and activities shape opportunity |
| 10 | Map the Future | Researches 3+ careers (salary, growth, requirements) | Builds a first Best-Fit List tied to career interests | Understands cost and ROI of pathways |
| 11 | Evaluate the Fit | Defines a Professional Aspiration aligned to priorities | Finalizes a personalized Best-Fit List with must-have criteria | Builds financial literacy (Funding 101) |
| 12 | The Final Mile | Validates the Professional Aspiration | Applies to 3+ best-fit institutions, apprenticeships, or workforce programs | Completes funding applications and confirms enrollment in a best-fit pathway |

A fourth strand, Purpose, Belonging and Agency, runs through all four years (self-identity and agency in 9th, growth mindset and self-advocacy in 10th, values and criteria in 11th, resilience for the transition in 12th).

**Whose curriculum is it, Willow's or OneGoal's? (Working)** One unified curriculum for FY28. Both libraries are being reviewed side by side and the strongest content wins; the test is that a student or teacher never sees a seam. The early hypothesis is that Willow's library is deeper in grades 9-10 and more student-facing and digital, and OneGoal is stronger on relational and identity-building work and on 11-12 list building. Milestones get locked first, then the modules and lessons that serve them. Kirat Doshi and Karla Robles-Reyes lead for OneGoal; Jaime Hudgins leads for Willow. Decisions to date are logged in the [curriculum decisions sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1qtdmBE1Mg8YhZV7Q2TAlS9PhQJnoJYzYsFCNA46o7R4/edit), and Jaime's [draft scope and sequence](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1XZfFbyZ5PO-N8abmtYYlQrz8XsDxGdhvTPHRvphO1D8/) is the working starting point.

**Is this just OneGoal switching to a new tech platform? (Settled)** No, OneGoal and Willow are building one combined 9-12 curriculum together, taught in the school day, on one platform, with OneGoal's training and implementation support around it. The platform is how the program is delivered; the curriculum, the advising model, and the implementation are what a school is buying.

**When will the curriculum be done? (Working; updated Sept 29)** The curriculum leads (Jaime for Willow; Kirat, Karla, and Anairis for OneGoal) are settling the foundational structure now and aim to have the shape of each grade (objectives, units, milestones) by mid-October. High-level scope and sequence, objectives, dosage, and sample content are due by about Nov 1 so P&G staff can be trained and start school conversations before master schedules are set. The full unified curriculum is built November through May: units, modules, and lesson objectives locked with most lessons built by end of March 2027, and all student- and teacher-facing lessons final by end of May 2027. Winter Solstice staff training (December 2026) is the first chance to bring OneGoal staff along.

**How much class time does it take? (Settled; Sept 17)** Every lesson comes in two versions, 30 minutes and 45 minutes. The minimum core experience is 30 minutes a week for 33 weeks in grades 9-10 (curriculum only, without advising), and 90 minutes a week for 33 weeks in grades 11-12. A school can spread those annual minutes across its schedule however it works best, and the class must meet at least weekly. Content is organized as core, depth, and supplemental, and the schedule is never planned to fill 100 percent of seat time, so testing, absences, and field trips don't knock a school off track.

**What implementation patterns can a school choose? (Settled)** Same core milestones, different paths. The cadences below are the schema's illustrative examples, not commitments:

| Pattern | Grades 9-10 | Grade 11 | Grade 12 | Best fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Graduated Intensity | 2x/week | 3x/week | Daily | Limited freshman/sophomore flexibility, more senior seat time |
| B. Senior Seminar Only | Not in program | Not in program | Daily | First-time partners, or schools that can only carve out senior year |
| C. Full Arc, Steady Cadence | 3x/week | 3x/week | 3x/week | Schools with an advisory period at every grade |
| D. Compressed Start | Not in program | 3x/week (heavier dosage) | Daily | Districts phasing in with upperclassmen first |

**Can a district start with senior year only? (Open)** The schema allows it (Pattern B) and lists it as a fit for first-time partners. OneGoal's curriculum lead prefers fewer low-dosage, foot-in-the-door starts in FY28 and clearer wins out of the gate, and the Sales & GTM draft treats senior-only as the pattern we steer away from toward 11-12 or 9-12. Partnership criterion 9 already requires every partner to build toward a strong 12th-grade experience within three years. Owner: Jenny, Kirat, and James at Workshop 3.

**Can a district start with 9th grade only? (Working)** Yes, and some do. These partners tend to be lower dosage and lower engagement, so treat it as a land-and-expand with a written path in the close plan, not the offer.

**Is the unit of sale a school, a district, or a grade level? (Working)** A grade level. A district can land with one grade level or one set of schools and expand on a written path. The push is whole-district first and whole-school second.

**Does the curriculum cover state-specific requirements (Texas HB8/CCMR and MyTexasFuture, NY STARS, MA MyCAP, Colorado's Big 3, California application platforms)? (Working; updated Sept 29)** Yes, that is our intention, and we don't have an exact plan yet. Curriculum is regionalized when a state's rules, aid, or content genuinely differ (in-state tuition, state aid, graduation requirements, a Colorado FAFSA lesson), not to accommodate local teaching preferences, and variants follow state lines rather than OneGoal's regional structure. State and local guidance inside the platform (in-state tuition, state aid, early-college incentives, local programs) is being built October through January, defaulting to the school's location so students don't re-enter it. Regional mandatory resources are being mapped now: NY STARS is expected to be a CSV data exchange; MyTexasFuture requirements are still unclear; California's application-platform lock-in is the harder case. Sellers get talking points on how the platform complements each state's tools.

**How much can a region or school customize? (Working)** Scope and sequence is mapped with each partner and lands about 90 percent the same across partners; the difference is dosage, format, and state-specific lessons. Semi-customization and mapping scope and sequence to a specific district is something we can sell and support, while being thoughtful about version control and scalability. The goal is more standardization and less preference-based customization, with thoughtful regionalization kept.

**Who teaches it and what does a lesson look like? (Settled)** Any educator: a teacher, a counselor, a CCR lead. Lessons are designed to make the teacher's job easier. Some lessons have students on the platform most of the period; in others only the exit ticket is on the platform. Students do a guided reflection with Alma after lessons, and teachers get summaries of exit tickets with exemplar and flagged responses surfaced. The 50-minute version asks for more products and projects; the 30-minute version asks for a paragraph instead of a page.

**What is Alma? (Settled)** Willow's AI advisor. Students use it for guided reflections after lessons and for career and pathway questions; staff use it to prepare for conversations. Alma is built on Anthropic's Claude with zero data retention and no training on student data. Every message passes a two-tier safety classifier; flagged conversations go to a human within 24 hours (see Technology below). Students can opt out and back in from settings; an opt-out degrades the guided reflections but everything else works. The student-facing line OneGoal uses: "This is not a private chat. Say something weird, we'll tell your teacher."

**Who does the advising, and where does it fit? (Working; updated Sept 29)** Advising is a team sport. Teachers and other trusted adults do most of the relationship-based advising; counselors keep the specialized work (transcripts, school forms, Common App processes, recommendations). Whole-class, small-group, and peer work all count as advising; one-to-one advising is a distinct, more individual piece the curriculum makes room for through independent and small-group work, without requiring every teacher to pull students during class. Advising can line up with milestones but never blocks a student from moving on in the curriculum. A minimum advising expectation (roughly quarterly was discussed, not agreed) is still being set. The advisor workspace (who to talk to and why, what the school already knows about the student, suggested questions, notes, and follow-up for the next adult) is built December through March.

**What is Year 13 and is it included? (Working)** Yes. Every student is paired with a OneGoal Postsecondary Advisor for a full year after graduation: student-centered advising on academics, finances, and wellbeing; a 24/7 advising chatbot (Mainstay) where a student can text #human to reach their advisor; and data-informed tiering from senior-year signals (academic match and completion supports) refined all year. In a Texas pilot, students with both a human advisor and the chatbot enrolled at 78 percent, against 59 percent with a human only, 50 percent with the chatbot only, and 25 percent with neither (descriptive, not causal). It is priced inside the seminar, never as a separate district line item, because districts cannot pay for students who have graduated. The delivery and funding model for FY28 is being designed.

**What is the evidence base? (Settled)** UChicago's Inclusive Economy Lab found OneGoal Fellows are 46 percent more likely to enroll, 47 percent more likely to persist, and 40 percent more likely to graduate than similar peers. Fellows hitting Quality Enrollment milestones complete at meaningfully higher rates (on-track Intent to Enroll: 40 percent completion vs. 25 percent off-track). Willow's platform supplies the completion and earnings data by program.

**Where do the career and ROI numbers come from? (Settled)** Career earnings use Department of Labor and O\*NET data at the city level, then state, then national. ROI uses federal college-level earnings from tax records, minus a state-localized high-school-graduate counterfactual over ten years, minus program cost; program-level ROI uses graduates of that program. A library of short day-in-the-life career videos sits alongside the assessment.

**What happens to Studio, MyOptions, DecidED, Canvas, and Mainstay? (Working)** Studio and MyOptions transition into the Willow platform. DecidED's knowledge (award-letter edge cases, affordability concepts) shapes Willow's financial-fit and award-letter comparison tools, built November through March, without assuming DecidED's code carries over. Canvas stays for legacy partners through the FY28 dual track and is retired as they convert or are no longer served; no curriculum work happens in Canvas going forward. Mainstay continues as the Year 13 chatbot.

**What does the educator get out of it? (Working)** Materials good enough that a teacher wants to use them, and a role in students' futures that teachers who have done it describe as feeding a part of their soul. A common misconception to correct early: districts often think they are buying this for their counselors. It is for every educator in the building, and the sales deck needs an educator value-proposition slide (Karla agreed Sept 10).

**Will there be journals or printed materials? (Settled)** Not in year one. Consumables are a Year 2 or 3 idea.

## Pricing, packaging, and contracting

**What is the offer? (Working)** One offer: the Classroom Experience as the schema defines it, sold by grade level, with training and support, progress monitoring, and Year 13 inside it. The four implementation patterns change the schedule, not the offer. Two hard gates: a protected block of at least 30 minutes, and the class taught at least weekly. More PD or coaching is sold on top; the offer stays one offer. Lighter and more intensive versions come later, once the base offer has sold enough times to learn from.

**How is it priced? (Working)** One structure for every district: a base price plus a per-student rate. Year 13 is priced inside the seminar and never appears as a district line item. Jenny's P&G team is drafting pricing guidance for the integrated model, and a price goes out the door from Workshop 3 (week of Oct 26). Until then, do not quote figures. Route pricing questions to Jenny, Jonathan Cowan, or Michael Stimpfel.

**What is the senior-seminar-only price? (Working)** Senior-seminar-only pricing is coming later. Until it is out, do not quote a figure. See the Open item on senior-only starts above.

**What else can I sell alongside it? (Working)** The schema's Additional Offerings stay as standalone, lower-cost products that build brand presence and open doors:

- Postsecondary Culture Survey for students (student and staff voice data with dashboards, a facilitated data dive, and action planning); a staff version pilots in FY27.
- Professional Learning for Counselors: nine competency-based modules, delivered in person (3 hours) or virtually (90 minutes), four-session minimum; a nationally hosted open-registration series is under consideration.

OnRamp curriculum PDFs, the Leadership Network, and standalone Studio are closed. Flagship is sold only as a step into the new model. Everything we sell returns data on outcomes.

**What support levels exist, and do districts pay for them? (Working)** A tiered readiness and support model: not ready, ready with heavy support, ready with medium support, ready with light support. Where a partner lands drives the support level and the price of any add-on PD or coaching. Enabling-conditions work is wanted by schools but rarely paid for directly, so the plan is two or three high-leverage resources (a master-schedule review, for example) that help a school get itself ready.

**What do I do when a district says it cannot afford it? (Working)** Intensify, don't discount. Add to the offer: a readiness engagement, heavier year-one support, a paid pilot, or an Additional Offering first. Bring philanthropy in early, from the first conversation, not when someone says they cannot pay. Nothing should be free; earned revenue has to grow, and we need schools and districts to put skin in the game (but we're happy for philanthropy to subsidize).

**Can philanthropy cover a district's first year? (Working)** Partly, and it should be planned from the first conversation. A pattern that works in California: philanthropy subsidizes much of year one, the school or district contributes something from the start, and the partner reaches full price by year three. Never zero; a district that pays something is a district that implements.

**What can districts pay with? (Working)** Perkins, Title I, Title II, Title IV-A, CTE allocations, state college-and-career funds (Texas HB3 pays districts directly for postsecondary success), local foundations, and a superintendent who finds money for what the board is excited about. Workforce money is a newer door: state workforce agencies and workforce boards will fund pathway work when we can show students' pathway interests by sector (WIOA training accounts are now approved at the high-school level in Illinois), and community colleges may co-fund through dual-credit arrangements. The moment a district is named, run the funding scan.

**Who contracts with the district? (Settled)** OneGoal. OneGoal keeps full ownership of district relationships, brand, and revenue. Willow provides the platform under the partnership.

**Who papers the data agreement? (Working; updated Sept 29)** Where a district has its own data-sharing agreement, that governs. Where it doesn't, OneGoal's standard data-sharing agreement is expected to apply, and the definitive agreement is being written so Willow's obligations mirror the district agreements OneGoal already holds. Willow also has an SDPC-modeled data privacy agreement template (data elements, subprocessor list, 72-hour breach notification, 30-day data return or destruction on termination). Final details land with the definitive agreement. Owner: Kait and Ryan.

**What does the district commit to? (Settled)** The nine partnership criteria from the schema:

1. Interested in giving at least the majority of a grade-level band time in the school day for postsecondary planning (a phased approach is fine).
2. A class block of at least 30 minutes; variable seat time supported.
3. The leader designates an adult to oversee school-wide implementation with OneGoal support.
4. The leader allocates staff to deliver the program.
5. Classroom Experience contract signed.
6. Willing and able to pay the agreed price.
7. Partner and OneGoal are a legal match (data, terms, conditions).
8. Agrees to use our technology to deliver the classroom experience.
9. Intends to build toward a strong 12th-grade experience within three years.

**How much staff time does a school commit? (Working; schema dial at 75%)** For an 11th and 12th grade implementation: school leader about 6 hours a year (setup and progress monitoring); school coordinator about 30 hours (setup, progress monitoring, check-ins with OneGoal, training, site-visit support, async work); each educator about 8 hours outside class (training plus grade-team meetings for 11th and 12th grade teachers).

**Is this a multi-year commitment? (Working)** The offer is built for it: criterion 9 asks every partner to reach a strong 12th-grade experience within three years, and land-and-expand paths are written into the close plan. Contract term length is a Workshop 3 decision.

**Is there a pilot option? (Working)** Yes, as a step into the full offer rather than a free trial. The aim is at least one pilot in every region starting in January 2027 on the shared approach, so districts and our own teams see it working before FY28. Pilots are small, paid, bound to the partnership criteria, and credited against the contract. What a pilot includes is still being defined by the program team.

**Can a district buy Willow directly, without OneGoal? (Open)** Willow sells its curriculum and platform to its own partners and is currently pitching in Texas, New York, California, Georgia, Massachusetts, and Illinois/Wisconsin. Willow's stated focus this year is helping OneGoal sell, handing its own market relationships to the regional EDs to co-sell; written territory rules and inbound handling are still to come. Until those rules exist, tell James or Jenny about any district where both organizations may be active.

**Why wouldn't a district just buy Willow? (Settled)** Because the curriculum and platform only produce outcomes when a school implements them well, and that is OneGoal's twenty years: readiness, training, grade-team support, progress monitoring, and Year 13. Willow would otherwise have to staff all of that itself. Willow earns the same whether it or OneGoal makes the sale, and its stated focus this year is helping OneGoal sell.

## Sales process, territories, and handoffs

**What is the sales process? (Working)** Four meetings, each with its own purpose and exit. Proposals stall; a plan built together in the room closes.

| Step | Length | Question it answers | What happens | Leaves with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Relationship conversation | 30 min, one-to-one, no deck | Right person, same beliefs? | Their journey and why they do this work; what is working and not; what has to be different next year; why now. Listen for belief, authority, appetite, ambition. | A counterpart and a Vision Conversation date, or an introduction. Disqualify early. |
| 1. Vision conversation | 45 min | Should we work on this together? | The ED's own vision for the region; the status quo; the district's uncomfortable truth in its own numbers, told in the metrics it is already held to. No demo. Where do they sit on the four stages? | A Readiness Session date and who has to be in the room. Never "stay in touch." |
| Pre-readiness call | 15 min, mandatory, with the champion | What does this room need to see? | Who is coming and what each cares about; the one slice of the Classroom Experience that matters here; what not to show; the unsaid objection. | The slice, the attendee map, a counterpart co-designing the session. |
| 2. Readiness session with a small demo | 60-75 min, everyone who has to say yes | Can we learn from each other while we do it? | Recap their words. The slice, and only the slice, for fifteen minutes with their real data. Then the Readiness Assessment live, to consensus. | A soft commitment to build the plan together. |
| 3. Outcomes and implementation session | 90 min, in person where possible | How do we create value before we sign? | A two-page working doc: the opportunity in their words, the outcomes they will hold us to, pattern and footprint, launch plan, roles and capacity ask, risks with a mitigation each, investment and the funding attached. | Launch plan, start date, and a close plan working backward from signature: who signs, board approval, procurement, DPA, source of funds. No proposal is ever sent. |

Between meetings: ask the wand question before ("if you could wave a wand, what would you want from this conversation?"), debrief with the counterpart within the hour after, move the counterpart to text, go see them when a deal stalls, and know the signatory before the final week.

**Do I lead with the demo? (Settled)** No. The demo is one of the few things a seller can hold back, and it is spent once shown. It comes in step 2, after the pre-readiness call tells you which slice this room needs. Shown early, people get lost in the parts they do not care about and miss the problem it solves for them.

**Do I lead with Willow? (Settled)** No. Willow is how we do it, not why a district should buy. Sell the vision, the outcomes, and twenty years of implementation. Technology is table stakes and does not deliver outcomes; every district leader knows you can buy the best tool and still fail at implementation. Leading with the platform invites "we already have Naviance."

**What do I say when they already have a platform (SchooLinks in CPS, Xello and Overgrad in NYC, Naviance or SCOIR everywhere)? (Working)** Not a feature comparison. We are a full curriculum and a full program, and the district's outcomes after five years on that platform are why we are talking. Then the practical layer: curriculum, career exploration, and best-fit list building happen in Willow; applications, FAFSA, and compliance records stay in the district's system of record; a formatted export moves the work across, with integration support priced in where nobody at the district can do it; and we share data back to the district immediately. Twelfth grade is where the legacy/compliance platform usually takes over (FAFSA, Common App), and that is fine. We will work together to approach legacy platform providers to make integrations.

Agreed Sept 22: do not pitch replacing the legacy platform at the door. ## Implementation and onboarding

**What does OneGoal's support look like once a school signs? (Working; schema dial at 75%)** Four parts:

1. Launch and progress monitoring: a structured planning process with school leadership before the year begins (curricular priorities, a shared scope and sequence, training and progress logistics), then twice-a-year check-ins with leadership on real-time dashboards, plus 1:1 time with the site coordinator.
2. Training: a beginning-of-year session for all participating staff and a mid-year session, covering both the classroom experience and the educators' own postsecondary-readiness knowledge.
3. Teacher supports: recurring grade-team meetings for 11th and 12th grade teachers, cadence tailored to the grade band, plus office hours in months with no meeting. 9th and 10th grade teachers get a PLC playbook and rely on the trainings.
4. Site visits: two in-person visits a year focused on how the program is running, not instructional coaching.

For an 11th and 12th grade implementation that is roughly 160 OneGoal hours a year per school: about 38 hours on setup and progress monitoring, 24 on training, 56 on grade-team support, 18 on site visits, plus a buffer.

**What is the readiness assessment, and how does it relate to the enabling conditions? (Working)** They are being merged into one tool. The enabling conditions (OneGoal's framework) name the structural and cultural conditions a school needs before it can launch. The transformation matrix (Willow's) is a four-stage, seven-dimension self-assessment: how coherent is your approach to career readiness today, what does every student receive, do you use labor-market data, are you honest with young people about outcomes. In the room, the district places itself on each row, to consensus. Landing at a stage tells the seller what the transformation looks like from here and what has to be true to launch. "Readiness assessment" is the external name; "enabling conditions" stays internal.

**What if a school is not ready? (Working)** If there is no in-school time yet, the schema offers a structured pre-implementation year ("Preparing for Impact") to clear the structural hurdles, and a readiness plan with two or three high-leverage resources such as a master-schedule review. An Additional Offering can come first. The offer itself waits until the criteria close.

**When can a new partner go live? (Working; updated Sept 29)** FY28 (school year 2027-28) is the launch for all new and ready flagship partners. Before that, the aim is at least one pilot per region starting in January 2027. Along the way: readiness work with schools (December to February), training and on-demand support built February to April, a practice environment for educators March to May, and a launch and progress-monitoring process for each school April to May. The product is ready for training-material development in June 2027 and school PD starts in July.

**What happens to current partners on Canvas and the old programs? (Settled; updated Sept 29)** There are no new Essentials sales; the integrated model on Willow is the offer. Flagship partners move to Willow and then to the new model. Some legacy partners stay on Canvas into FY28 because of district contracts, approvals, or IT cycles; running the old stack into FY29 is a conservative fallback, not the plan. A legacy migration plan (who moves when, what stays online in maintenance mode, which district approvals block a move, when tools retire) is built January to April, and conversion conversations run January to March. No new curriculum work happens in Canvas.

**Who is the school-based coordinator, and what do they do? (Settled)** A teacher leader, CCR head, or lead counselor the school leader designates (criterion 3). About 30 hours a year: setup, progress monitoring, six check-ins with OneGoal, training, site-visit support, and async work.

**What training do educators get, and how? (Working; updated Sept 29)** Beginning-of-year and mid-year sessions, role-specific launch training, and on-demand support inside the platform: a two-minute explanation, a script, or the relevant OneGoal training resource at the moment an educator needs it, including in the advisor workspace. OneGoal's training team owns the content; Willow surfaces it. No separate LMS is required. Jaime and Alysa work directly with the program leads and implementers closest to schools, with OneGoal's training team built in, so guidance doesn't get diluted through layers; EDs are not the training channel for school staff. OneGoal's own staff training runs through Winter Solstice (December 2026) and a spring enablement push.

**What does progress monitoring look like? (Working)** A school lead tracks participation and outcomes across the school; automated reports go to leadership through the year; leadership check-ins twice a year on real dashboards. Admin monitoring layers are being built for the program lead, the school leader, and the district. The KPI set is being trimmed to what scales; OneGoal currently monitors more than is sustainable.

**Who does what between OneGoal and Willow during implementation? (Working)** OneGoal owns the relationship, planning with leadership, training, grade-team support, site visits, and Year 13. Willow owns the platform: provisioning, rostering, integrations, technical support, safety monitoring, and the curriculum build. The real-time technical support model for educators (who a coordinator calls, response times) is still being defined. Owner: Jaime and Alysa, with Ryan.

## Technology, data, privacy, and integrations

The test Jonathan set on Sept 18 applies to every answer here: if we say rostering is automatic, it has to be automatic; if we say it is interoperable, it has to be. Where today's reality differs from where we are headed, both are stated.

**Rostering and single sign-on: Clever and ClassLink? (Working; updated Sept 29)** Google sign-in and Clever are live and set up for districts today (Clever runs as OAuth with just-in-time provisioning on first login). In some districts SSO is a non-negotiable before anything else, so raise it early. Enterprise login work runs January to May: ClassLink where a district requires it (Houston likely will), and SAML for districts that need it, including Microsoft Entra. Roster import also works from a CSV (first name, last name, email, external ID, grade, class); OneRoster formats are a scoped build if a district prefers them. Roster sync is currently a job Willow's team triggers rather than a nightly schedule; converting it to a scheduled sync is a small change made when a partner needs it. Do not describe rostering as fully automatic until that is done.

**Positioning next to CCR platforms with multi-year contracts (Xello, SchooLinks, Naviance, Overgrad, SCOIR)? (Working; updated Sept 22)** Willow does not compete with them on workflow; it competes on curriculum and the instructional experience around career exploration. Coexistence is the answer: OneGoal and Willow for the 9-11 curriculum and list building, the legacy platform for 12th-grade application management, and as little double entry as possible. The near-term mechanism is a Willow export pre-formatted for upload into each major platform (a small lift on Willow's side). Overgrad is the platform Willow meets most often in charter networks and there is an existing, lightly used API: Willow can push a student's balanced list into Overgrad's follow list, or a school can have students re-enter their Willow list in Overgrad as a quality check; both work. Deeper integrations come district by district and vendor by vendor, and a customer asking their vendor for API access is what moves them. OneGoal, not Willow, asks the friendly platforms for integrations, and OneGoal staff never hold credentials to a school's Overgrad or SchooLinks. Platform-specific one-pagers for the top four systems are coming.

**Does Willow work with Common App? (Working)** Yes. Students are using Willow's Common App integration today for the application work itself. When a school also runs Xello or another platform that connects to Common App, the school picks one system for 12th-grade applications; in practice that is often the legacy platform, with OneGoal and Willow carrying grades 9-11 and list building.

**SIS and gradebook (PowerSchool, Infinite Campus, Skyward)? (Working; updated Sept 29)** SIS data reaches Willow through Clever: Willow receives whatever the district chooses to share in its Clever setup. Whether GPA and grades come through depends on that setup, and Ryan is confirming the details. Where OneGoal already holds authoritative school data, such as a clean district GPA file, OneGoal loads it into Willow. Direct SIS integrations get built only for a demonstrated district need. Say "rostering and SIS data through Clever, and we will scope anything deeper with your IT team."

**LMS integration (Google Classroom, Canvas, Schoology)? (Working; updated Sept 22)** Willow is its own LMS: the curriculum, portfolio work, and rich content live inside the platform, and there are no external LMS connections today. LMS integration is on the roadmap. The question comes up mostly because OneGoal delivers on Canvas today; districts without an LMS habit rarely ask. Lessons can be downloaded as Google Doc or Word files for districts that need materials in their own system.

**Does DecidED still work? (Settled)** Yes. Willow uses the DecidED API for financial-aid analysis, so the financial-fit function continues inside the platform.

**How is student data protected? (Settled)** Willow operates as a "school official" under FERPA: no redisclosure, no advertising, no resale, no training AI on student data. An SDPC-modeled data privacy agreement is ready with the data elements and subprocessor list as exhibits; 72-hour breach notification; data returned or destroyed within 30 days of termination; backups purged at 90 days. Every staff read or write of student personal information is logged for audit. Direct client access to the database is blocked; everything goes through the API.

**What does the AI see? (Settled)** Alma's context is limited to first and last name, grade, school, city and state, GPA, career interests and goals, personality and work-style results, the student's "My Why," and summaries of recent reflections. It never sees email, phone, ID numbers, medical information, parent contact details, income, free-or-reduced-lunch status, or ethnicity. AI conversations are hard-deleted after 24 months; a district can request earlier deletion.

**Which AI models, and is student data used to train them? (Settled)** Alma runs on Anthropic's Claude, chosen for its safety tuning for minors, with zero data retention and no training on inputs or outputs. Remaining OpenAI workloads (classification, embeddings) are on an API tier with training disabled and are being migrated off. No analytics trackers run in the platform.

**What happens if a student says something concerning to Alma? (Working; updated Sept 29)** Every message passes a two-tier safety classifier (pattern matching, then an AI classifier) for self-harm, violence, bullying, crisis, hate speech, dropout risk, and inappropriate content. Flags carry a severity and are triaged by a human within 24 hours, and a school's counseling team is engaged on critical flags. A health check pages Willow's team if the classifier stops running. Today Willow staff review the flag queue; the plan is that serious concerns route directly to the responsible adults at the school, as part of the district AI work finishing before the 2027-28 school year. Whether OneGoal staff also see safety flags is an open legal and operational decision for Garcia, legal, and program leads. Immigration status is not currently in the flag taxonomy and is under review.

**Can a student, school, or district turn off the AI? (Settled; updated Sept 29)** Today, an individual student can opt out from settings and opt back in; the guided reflections get simpler but everything else works. By the 2027-28 school year, a district or school will be able to turn student-facing AI off entirely. When it is off, students get a basic version of those moments (for example, the reflection question with a normal text response) rather than a second product, and AI-driven features such as personalized career and program recommendations and AI feedback on student work are not available. Willow's full experience uses AI, so sellers should explain what a school gives up. OneGoal is building an overarching program consent process as part of the Gates "human in the loop" advising work; parent-level permissions get built only where a district requires them.

**What about NYC and other districts with AI restrictions? (Working)** NYC prohibits AI "counseling" without defining it, and it is the number-one policy risk for the partnership; OneGoal is waiting on NYC DOE's final guidance before settling its posture, and how Alma's function is characterized is a live question with OneGoal's security team. LAUSD is near no-screens for high schoolers. Sellers in NY, CA, and other restricted districts should raise this with Kait or Ryan before promising the AI layer. The platform works with Alma off, and by the 2027-28 school year a district or school can switch student-facing AI off entirely.

**What is the security posture? (Working; updated Sept 29)** Willow's SOC 2 audit starts Oct 1, 2026, with completion targeted for February 2027, including role and access-control checks, an external penetration test, and multi-factor authentication for anyone with privileged administrative access. MFA is not required for students or regular teachers unless a district asks for it. Separately, OneGoal's security team (Jon Garcia, with OneGoal's engineering contractors) has reviewed Willow's answers and code, and the two teams are agreeing a remediation list ranked by real district and contractual risk; Willow is already shipping fixes. District-specific security requirements should go to Garcia early, especially in California, New York, and Chicago, which are strictest.

**Can a district turn off or monitor teacher-student messaging? (Open)** Chicago Public Schools has raised it. Whether switching messaging off is enough, or a district needs administrator visibility, is being worked out; there is no commitment to monitoring tools. Owner: Ryan and Garcia.

**Is it accessible, and does it work in other languages? (Working)** The core experience is being built to the applicable accessibility standard and toward the 2027 requirements (built November to March): new curriculum accessible by design, captions and descriptions on media where required, clear reading levels, and text structured for browser translation. Students can talk with Alma in other languages. Full translation of the platform, curriculum, and videos is not part of FY28.

**What are the uptime and incident commitments? (Working)** A public status page at [status.willowed.org](https://status.willowed.org) covers web, API, auth, Alma, and Clever sync. Incident response has three severity tiers with a 15-minute acknowledgment on the most severe, named playbooks for provider outages, and a blameless postmortem within five business days. A contractual uptime SLA for districts is not yet written. Owner: Ryan, with the definitive agreement.

**What data does the district get back? (Working; updated Sept 22)** Schools own their data, and Willow passes it back through a secure CSV or Clever's secure file transfer. In practice that means progress-monitoring dashboards, automated reports to leadership, pre-formatted exports for legacy systems, and, from Year 13, enrollment and persistence trends. NYC's current RFP asks specifically how data is returned to the district, so have this answer ready there. Aggregate, anonymized learning data across partners is never sold on its own.

**What devices and access do students need? (Open)** Not documented in the sources for this FAQ. Owner: Ryan.

**Is there a sandbox for a district's IT team? (Settled)** Yes: a separate QA environment on isolated infrastructure where test users, classes, and API credentials can be provisioned with no risk to production data.
